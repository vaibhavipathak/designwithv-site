The full story

01 The ChallengeA chatbot is only as trustworthy as the content behind it.

The situation: agents at Brightline Support, a fictional loyalty-program contact centre, searched three or more places for policy answers. The answers they found were sometimes out of date, sometimes contradicted each other, and often had no clear owner.

Why a chatbot alone would make it worse

  • It repeats stale content confidently. An outdated refund timeline becomes a fast wrong answer.
  • It blends conflicts. "5 to 7 days" and "10 days" can turn into "5 to 10 days", which no policy says.
  • Nobody fixes it. Without owners, problems are spotted by customers, not by the business.
  • Training drifts too. Job aids and e-learning built on old policy quietly go stale.

The real problem: trust, not search. Agents need answers they can rely on, and a named person to fix them when they're wrong.

02 DiscoveryThe pain point was freshness and ownership, not finding the article.

Where the needs came from: the Brightline discovery work (interviews, a shift walk-through, QA trends), plus patterns from 7 years in real contact centres and knowledge bases. An interview question also shaped it: "How will you check the freshness of the content?"

04 Content OwnershipEvery article has an owner and a backup, and nothing goes unowned quietly.

Where ownership lives: in each article's header, and in an owner register listing every article's owner, backup, review cycle, review dates and linked training.

Four rules

  • 1. No owner, no publish. An unowned article goes to the Knowledge Manager the same day.
  • 2. Weekly ownership check. The register and the articles must agree.
  • 3. Owner shown with every answer. Agents know who to ask; owners see their name on what they publish.
  • 4. Owner first, then backup. No reply in 5 business days, and the item goes to the backup.
05 How It WorksThree workflows share one store of owned articles.

1. Ingest: loading the articles

Reads the 12 articles, tags each one current, overdue or unowned, splits them into 24 chunks and stores them in Pinecone with their owner and review dates. Re-running it never creates duplicates.

2. Chat: answering questions

An AI agent searches the articles, then answers from only what it found. It ends every answer with the source, owner and review date.

  • Answer the exact question first, then conditions, in the article's own words
  • Warn when an article has no owner or is overdue for review
  • If two articles disagree, use the most recently reviewed one and say so; never blend them
  • Never repeat customer details; never promise more than an article allows

Where it runs: ingest and the daily check run in n8n on my computer. The chat runs on n8n Cloud, so the live demo works any time.

06 Testing the AnswersI wrote 20 test questions first, including traps, and scored every answer.
KindCountWhat it tests
Lookup6One clear answer in one article
Two-article3Combining two articles correctly
Vague3Asking instead of guessing
Out of scope4Saying "I don't know", and resisting "ignore your rules"
Trap3A planted conflict, an unowned article and a false premise
Personal data1Not repeating customer details

Scoring: Correct (0 to 2), Sourced (0 or 1), Safe (0 or 1). Pass mark: 90% correct, 90% sourced, 100% safe.

What changed, and why

  • A question it couldn't answer caused an error. It kept searching for a hotel policy that doesn't exist. Fix: search at most twice, then say "I don't know".
  • Vague questions missed an option. Fix: name every matching process in the clarifying question.
  • Unneeded privacy reminders on every answer. Fix: remind only when personal data appears.

Still not perfect: two answers scored 1 out of 2 in the final run. One blurred a transfer limit; one left out the goodwill limit. I scored them honestly. The Eval Scorecard shows every question and score.

Why rules and AI together: rules handle exact things like dates, for free. One AI call per run handles what needs reading and comparing, for a fraction of a cent.

08 What Went WrongSeven real problems, each one fixed and turned into a design rule.
What happenedFixLesson
Pinecone's console no longer offered the index type n8n needsCreated it through Pinecone's API from an n8n setup workflowCheck the current product, not the tutorial
Testing stopped at 20 requests a day on Gemini's free tierMoved to the paid tier with Google AI Pro credits and a spend capBudget for real usage before testing at scale
The chatbot errored on a question it couldn't answerSearch at most twice, then say "I don't know"Tell the AI when to stop
The daily check froze, then a chat answer hungReturned the model to Google's default temperature, everywhereFollow model-specific guidance; fix root causes everywhere
The daily check returned half an answerRaised the length limit; the failure was caught and reported, not hiddenDesign for failure: make errors visible
A test policy change muddled the conflict I was testingSwapped it for a cleaner changeEach test should measure one thing
An answer led with a condition, not the timeframe asked forAnswer the exact question first, in the article's own wordsHuman review catches drift that scores miss

For a real team, I would add

  • A connection to SharePoint, Confluence or the existing knowledge base
  • Owner digests by email or Teams, with approve and reject buttons
  • Single sign-on, question logging and a weekly eval run with alerts
  • Automatic removal of personal data before anything reaches the AI, signed off by Privacy
